C1. Survey band

The map band keeps the graticule and your precinct's shape, then closes with a NEATLINE: a fine rule with survey tick marks, the way a printed map's edge is drawn. Text below sits on clean paper. The tick detail is small but it is what makes the split feel like cartography instead of a divider.

The whole widget is the map: graticule edge to edge, your precinct drawn as large as it can go. The numbers sit on a bordered paper PLATE, the way labels sit on printed maps, so text never touches the grid. The shape gets maximum billing here.

My read this round: C3 (side split) is the most functional; the shape always has its own panel at a fixed size, nothing overlaps, and the right column mirrors the app's hero, but the pin loses its corner on the small size. C2 (label plate) shows the shape biggest and looks the most like a real map, at the cost of tighter text; note how the plate handles R+91 without breaking. C1 (survey band) is the closest evolution of today's widget, and the tick-marked neatline is the kind of drawn detail that reads hand-made rather than generated.
